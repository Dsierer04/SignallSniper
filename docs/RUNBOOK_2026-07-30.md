# Runbook — Thursday, 2026-07-30

Tomorrow is unusually dense: a macro triple at the open and two of the largest
companies on earth reporting after the close, the day after an FOMC decision.

| Time (ET) | Event | Playable? |
|---|---|---|
| 08:30 | Q2 GDP advance, PCE deflator, initial claims | **No** — see below |
| 09:30 | Open, post-FOMC drift | Setup only |
| 16:05–16:35 | **AAPL** and **AMZN** 8-K / press release | **Yes — hop 1** |
| 17:00 | Both earnings calls | **Yes — slowest, richest** |

## What is not playable, and why

**08:30 GDP/PCE.** This is the single most contested microsecond in macro. The
number is released to colocated subscribers and the rates complex reprices before
your packet leaves your ISP. Do not trade the print.

What you *can* do is trade the **propagation** afterward. `MACRO_RATES` links in
`market/linkage.py` map a rates move onto XLRE / KRE / IWM / XHB / XLU, which
reprice on a 30–90s lag because the mapping is an inference, not a quote. That
window is real but it is thinner than the earnings window — it's a warm-up, not
the main event.

**AAPL/AMZN headline EPS at 16:05.** Same story. The wire feed hits colocated
subscribers first and the mega cap reprices in under 50ms. The engine will
*refuse* to signal here and that refusal is correct — `evaluate_primary` computes
residual = expected − already_priced and returns `None` once the name has moved.
You can verify this: `python3 -m signalsniper demo` shows AAPL rejected with
`residual_below_min` while the suppliers signal.

## What is playable

### The 16:05 supplier dislocation — this is the trade

AAPL prints. AAPL moves instantly. **Cirrus Logic does not.** CRUS books ~90% of
its revenue from Apple, but nobody is standing on the CRUS book at 16:06 — the
inference "Apple guided hardware down, therefore the audio codec supplier is
worth less" takes a human two to ten minutes, and after-hours books are thin.

The engine reads the magnitude off AAPL's tape rather than predicting it, so it
doesn't need to be right about how big the news was — only about who is
downstream of it. See `signal/engine.py::evaluate_second_order`.

Pre-armed complexes:

```bash
python3 -m signalsniper links AAPL
python3 -m signalsniper links AMZN
python3 -m signalsniper links MACRO_RATES
```

**Channel gating matters here.** A Services beat propagates to GOOGL (search TAC)
and the app economy; it must *not* propagate to RF front-end suppliers. A
hardware guide-down does the reverse. The classifier picks the channel off the
release text — so the release text has to actually get read, which is what the
17:00 window is for.

**Don't forget the inverse edges.** AMZN crushing retail is *bad* for SHOP, ETSY,
W and bad for FDX/UPS parcel volume. The reflexive trade is to buy everything
adjacent to a winner; the inverse links are where the least competition is.

### The 17:00 calls — slowest and richest

Guidance revisions on the call move stocks more than the print does, and they
unfold over sixty minutes. This is a human-speed game where a fast structured
listener genuinely competes. Segment numbers (AWS growth rate, Services margin,
capex guide) land here and reprice the whole downstream complex a second time.

The AWS growth rate and the capex guide are the two numbers with the widest
second-order fan-out — capex directly reprices VRT / ETN / GEV / PWR, and those
names are followed by nobody who is also watching an Amazon call.

## Timeline

### Tonight

```bash

python3 -m pytest -q                  # expect 125 passed

```

Then decide the two things that actually determine tomorrow:

1. **Market data feed.** If you're on Alpaca `iex`, the 16:05 window will produce
   confident nonsense — IEX is ~2% of consolidated volume and after-hours prints
   are too thin to reference against. Get SIP or a Massive real-time plan, or sit
   out the earnings window and trade only the 09:30–16:00 session.
2. **Position size.** Set `EQUITY` honestly. `RISK_PER_TRADE=0.01` and
   `MAX_DAILY_LOSS=0.03` are the defaults and they are not conservative —
   they're the reason a bad read costs you 3% instead of the account.

### 08:00 ET

```bash
python3 -m signalsniper watch        # alert-only, no --live
```

Watch the macro complex propagate off the 08:30 print. **Trade nothing.** You're
calibrating: do the `MACRO_RATES` alerts fire on names that then actually move?
If the alerts are noise on a day you can observe cheaply, that tells you
something important before 16:05.

### 15:45 ET

Re-check the tape has depth on the whole linked universe — second-order signals
are impossible without quotes on the *linked* names, not just the issuers. The
heartbeat log prints tick counts per symbol.

```
heartbeat counts={'docs': N, 'events': N, 'signals': N} feeds={...} risk={...}
```

If `CRUS`, `SWKS`, `ANET`, `VRT` show near-zero ticks, your subscription isn't
covering them and the 16:05 window is dead. Fix it now, not at 16:04.

### 16:00 ET

Decide `--live` or not. If you haven't watched a full session of alerts and
agreed with them, stay alert-only tonight and paper the whole thing. The system
will still be here Friday, and Friday has the same structure with less crowding.

### 16:05–16:35 ET

Alerts fire. For each one, before acting:

- Does the linkage note describe a real economic exposure, or a vibe?
- Is the primary's move a real repricing, or a thin after-hours print on two
  hundred shares?
- What's the spread? The engine caps at 60bps, but after hours you should want
  much tighter than that on anything you actually cross.

The engine's `notes` field carries the full arithmetic — `implied=-357bps
actual=-60bps slack=83%`. If that arithmetic doesn't survive ten seconds of your
own scrutiny, skip it. The system's job is to find candidates fast; yours is to
veto.

### After the close

```bash
python3 -m signalsniper demo   # sanity
```

Write down every alert, whether you took it, and what happened over the next
thirty minutes. **The betas in `market/linkage.py` are priors, not truth** —
they're read off disclosed revenue-concentration figures and they are the first
thing you should recalibrate against real fills. One night of honest logging is
worth more than any amount of additional feature work.

## Kill conditions

Stop for the day, no negotiation, if any of these:

- `MAX_DAILY_LOSS` trips. The kill switch is sticky by design and only
  `resume()` clears it. Don't clear it.
- Two consecutive signals where the primary move reversed within 60s — that means
  you're reading noise prints, not repricings.
- The heartbeat shows feed errors climbing or SEC 429s. A rate-limited feed makes
  you *late*, which is worse than being absent.
- You find yourself wanting to trade the 08:30 or 16:05 headline directly.

## Honest expectations

The realistic distribution for one well-executed session on this: a handful of
signals, of which maybe two are clean, on a few thousand dollars of notional per
position at the default risk settings. That's a few hundred to low thousands on a
good night and a capped ~3% loss on a bad one.

The path to more is more *events*, not more *size* — the same machinery pointed
at the 8-K long tail across the whole market every day, where the competition is
genuinely thin. Tomorrow is a good day to find out whether the linkage betas are
any good, on the two names where the propagation is most legible.
