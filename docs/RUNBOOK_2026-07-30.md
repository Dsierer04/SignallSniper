## What is not playable, and why

**08:30 GDP/PCE.** This is the single most contested microsecond in macro. The
number is released to colocated subscribers and the rates complex reprices before
your packet leaves your ISP. Do not trade the print.

What you *can* do is trade the **propagation** afterward. `MACRO_RATES` links in
`market/linkage.py` map a rates move onto XLRE / KRE / IWM / XHB / XLU, which
reprice on a 30–90s lag because the mapping is an inference, not a quote. That
window is real but it is thinner than the earnings window — it's a warm-up, not
the main event.

**AAPL/AMZN headline EPS at 16:05.** Same story. The wire feed hits colocated
subscribers first and the mega cap reprices in under 50ms. The engine will
*refuse* to signal here and that refusal is correct — `evaluate_primary` computes
residual = expected − already_priced and returns `None` once the name has moved.
You can verify this: `python3 -m signalsniper demo` shows AAPL rejected with
`residual_below_min` while the suppliers signal.

## The extended-hours constraint (read before 16:00)

**Alpaca rejects bracket orders outside regular hours.** After 16:00 you get
limit orders only, DAY/GTC, `extended_hours=true`. No bracket, no OCO.

So on the 16:05 trade — the one this whole system is for — **there is no
broker-side stop.** The stop lives in the daemon. If the process dies, the laptop
sleeps, or wifi drops, you are naked in a name that just moved several percent.

Mitigations, all already wired:

- `ALLOW_EXTENDED=1` is a separate opt-in from `LIVE_TRADING=1`. Both needed.
- The runner tracks client-side-stop tickers and **flattens them on shutdown**
  before tearing down.
- A failed flatten logs CRITICAL telling you to close manually.
- `python3 -m signalsniper flatten` is the panic button. Know it beforehand.

Operationally: wired internet, sleep disabled, broker UI open in a tab. If you
can't guarantee those, trade the regular session only.

## What is playable

### The 16:05 supplier dislocation — UNPROVEN, do not fund it blind

This was pitched as the trade. Verification did not support it.

The claim was that CRUS, booking ~90% of revenue from Apple, does not reprice
when Apple does, because the inference takes a human minutes. The evidence says
the inference is machine-mediated and near-simultaneous, and that the window for
delayed participants collapsed from ~10 seconds pre-2016 to roughly zero. The
monthly-horizon papers everyone cites for this (Cohen & Frazzini, Menzly & Ozbas)
contain no intraday analysis at all.

**The way to settle it for your names is to measure it:**

```bash
python3 tools/validate.py --primary AAPL --feed sip
```

That reports, per linked name across 10 historical Apple prints, what fraction of
its eventual move was still available five minutes in. If CRUS shows 60%+ still
available and actually printed in that window, you have evidence the general
literature does not — trade it small. If it shows 5%, the name repriced with
Apple and there was never anything to catch.

The original reasoning, kept so the claim can be judged on its merits:

The engine reads the magnitude off AAPL's tape rather than predicting it, so it
doesn't need to be right about how big the news was — only about who is
downstream of it. See `signal/engine.py::evaluate_second_order`.

Pre-armed complexes:

```bash
python3 -m signalsniper links AAPL
python3 -m signalsniper links AMZN
python3 -m signalsniper links MACRO_RATES
```

**Channel gating matters here.** A Services beat propagates to GOOGL (search TAC)
and the app economy; it must *not* propagate to RF front-end suppliers. A
hardware guide-down does the reverse. The classifier picks the channel off the
release text — so the release text has to actually get read, which is what the
17:00 window is for.

**The inverse edges I pitched were wrong.** I claimed AMZN strength was bad for
SHOP/ETSY/W and for FDX/UPS, and that those were "where the least competition
is." Fact-check: SHOP-AMZN correlation is *positive* at every horizon (+0.45 3m,
+0.42 1y, +0.59 5y). Those links are removed. The one inverse link that survived
is TTD — Amazon's ad business is genuinely its biggest competitive threat.

### The 17:00 calls — the actual contention window

Note the correction above: the press releases are staggered (AMZN ~16:00, AAPL
16:30), so the PR cascade is sequential and the system has ~30 minutes between
them. It is the **calls** that run concurrently at 17:00.

Verify Amazon's call time against their IR page before the session — if it is
actually 17:30 the two do not overlap at all and the contention concern
disappears.


Guidance revisions on the call move stocks more than the print does, and they
unfold over sixty minutes. This is a human-speed game where a fast structured
listener genuinely competes. Segment numbers (AWS growth rate, Services margin,
capex guide) land here and reprice the whole downstream complex a second time.

The AWS growth rate and the capex guide are the two numbers with the widest
second-order fan-out — capex directly reprices VRT / ETN / GEV / PWR, and those
names are followed by nobody who is also watching an Amazon call.

## Kill conditions

Stop for the day, no negotiation, if any of these:

- `MAX_DAILY_LOSS` trips. The kill switch is sticky by design and only
  `resume()` clears it. Don't clear it.
- Two consecutive signals where the primary move reversed within 60s — that means
  you're reading noise prints, not repricings.
- The heartbeat shows feed errors climbing or SEC 429s. A rate-limited feed makes
  you *late*, which is worse than being absent.
- You find yourself wanting to trade the 08:30 or 16:05 headline directly.

## Honest expectations

Revised after verification.

The second-order trade has no measured edge behind it. Its expected value is
unknown and the published evidence points against it at this timescale. Sizing it
as though it were an edge is how a variance-only strategy drains an account
slowly while individual wins make it feel like it works.

What is defensible tomorrow:

- **Alert-only across all sessions, logging everything.** One night of data on
  your specific names is worth more than the whole literature review, because it
  is the only evidence that is actually about *your* universe and *your* fills.
- **The EDGAR long tail**, if you want to trade. That is a different mechanism —
  reading speed on a small-cap 8-K or 424B5 that has no algo coverage, not
  cross-firm inference. It was not refuted because it was not what the research
  examined.
- **Nothing on the 08:30 print or the 16:05 headline.** Unchanged and correct.

The path forward is measurement, not size. Run `tools/validate.py` tonight; it
answers in twenty minutes what a quarter of trading would answer expensively.
